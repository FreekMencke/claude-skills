# Case notes

What the skill's steps did on one typeface project, and which attempts failed, so a session
changing a step can see why it is the way it is. SKILL.md points here from each step.

The family: three drawn masters (Light 300, Regular 400, Bold 700), whole-unit outlines
converted from TrueType, Light as the shape reference; Thin (100) and Black (900) derived
from them, at stems 28.4 and 145 (glyph n). k is the stem ratio to the nearest drawn
master (Thin 28.4 / Light 61.1 = 0.465). Counts are from `audit.py`.

## Choosing the target stems

A Black at 153 had 38 blocks in 14 glyphs, at 145 19 blocks in 7 (™, ‰, &, @ and } broke only above about 150); 145 also made the steps Bold–ExtraBold–Black as even as Regular–Bold.

## The contrast step

At Thin it took blocks from 40 (in 14 glyphs) to 15 (in 5), strokes out of proportion from 72 to 7, with f and t crossbars at 18, € bars 19, the end of !'s stem 15, N's notch at 35 (Light × ratio: 32), the top of 4's counter at 669 against 674, v, w, < and the * arms within 1 unit of Light × ratio along their length, and v's crotch at 19 (Light × ratio 19). At Black it widened ™ by 30 and ‰ by 27 and otherwise did little: the problems there are segments passing zero at joins and terminals.

How it got there, so the next change doesn't undo it. Its first version moved corner points on their own; a reviewer found leaning stems (N, M, 4) and bars turned into wedges (Z, 2, z, 7, ®, $, €). The audit now flags both (`edge-turned`, `stroke-wedge`; 0 and 2 after plain extrapolation, 73 and 38 after that version), and `audit.edge_measures` gives the raw numbers the step uses to refuse any change that makes them worse. Its second version still moved a collapsed corner on its own as well as setting the strokes, and a second review found v, w, W, <, > and ^ 15–40% heavier than x, y and k: moving a corner alone thickens the strokes around it. Setting the strokes first fixed the weight, but then the crotches of v and w and the tips of < and > collapsed (a third review): with the strokes at their thickness, the extrapolated foot was too wide for the corner to sit higher. Moving both strokes as a whole narrows the foot instead (v's foot 45 → 32 units, Light × ratio 32). A corner moves on its own only when it is still pushed through the far side (^, whose inner apex poked through the top).

## Recipes

How the recipes were organised, and it worked: one recipe per review round (groups 1 to 9), each run after the ones before it; each round's folder holds its recipe, a zip of the sources after it, the built fonts, the audit, the build check and the sheets the user judged. A rebuild script derives from the drawn masters and runs the contrast step, collinear and every recipe in order (one rebuild script per round, each running the one before it and then its own round's recipe; the last ran group 7's kerning floor after the outline recipes of groups 8 and 9, since it measures the final outlines); check that it reproduces the last round exactly before trusting it. After any change to `reshape.py` itself, run every recipe again from the start and compare each rebuilt glyph's checks and filled render with the round before: a fix aimed at one glyph moved others (a new direction estimate for the C took & from 3 slows to 11; the first slide rule for Black & put a step on B's waist).

After the contrast step and collinear, a recipe of 14 calls on 19 glyph/weights (ð, the dieresis dots, two.tf, the capital circumflexes, tilde and cedilla, r, %, f, S and s at Thin; the braces, the arrow and f at Black) took the audit from 33 blocks and 923 slows to 25 and 882, changed only those glyphs, and left every advance width and all five masters' compatibility as they were. stroke-weight went from 4 glyphs to 0, and stroke-uneven lost S, s and r. The S's two blocks (a fold where the spine meets the lower bowl) went with `rebuild-stroke`, its slows from 18 to 10. Measured: the S's stroke went from x0.53-1.9 of Light x k to about 1.0 all round; the hook of f from a pointed counter and x0.76 at the shoulder to x0.97-1.05.

A second recipe (group 2: the 3 and three.tf, ? and ¿ at both weights, zero against zero.tf at Black, six at Thin; 10 glyph/weights) took it on to 14 blocks and 826 slows. The folds in the necks of ? and ¿ went (? at Thin: 3 blocks and 18 slows to 0 and 2), the 3 went from x0.57-1.48 along its stroke to even (8 slows to 3 at Thin), and copy-drift to none. For the 3, rebuilding its two bowls separately, with the waist kept, thinned the joint to x0.25: one stroke, outside against inside, worked. At Black the inside had to stop a node short of the bar, or the kept bar end turned the next segment round. Rounding bends short fitted handles several degrees (a corner-flip at Thin ?): `--realign`.

A third recipe (group 3: & and ¢ at Thin, ¢, $ and € at Black, 5 glyph/weights) took the last blocks away: 0 blocks and 815 slows, against 33 and 923 before the first recipe, every advance width as it was. The designer's review of the result, glyph by glyph in filled renders, then drove six more rounds on &, ¢, c and C at Thin (lessons below); final: & 0/3 checks, ¢ 0/3, c and C 0/0, the whole audit 0 blocks and 802 slows, widths unchanged.

What didn't work there, so the next attempt can skip it: `strokes` on d and nine at Thin (the weight sits at the corner nodes where the bowl meets the stem or the tail, and moving them kinked the curve: nine's junction stayed x1.40, with three new slows; `rebuild-outline` fixed both in group 4); moving every edge of a chevron in by the same amount (the apex came out half as deep as Light's proportions: use `chevron`); moving the corner nodes of a stroke one by one (each move kinked the curves next to it; the Thin braces' lopsided point needed `rebuild-outline --smooth-centre 2`); smoothing a lumpy derived centre line (it evened nothing and pulled the stroke ends in: use `--centre ref`); `offset-inner` on Black's f (the hook is too heavy for a wider inside radius; it added four slows, so `upright` evened it instead); trying another extrapolation method for the glyph (piecewise, fit or wide: all as uneven or worse before the contrast step). A rebuild that follows the master can move a stroke end (Thin S's lower terminal reached 21 units further left, as in Light): `--keep-limits` keeps the bowls' extremes and overshoots, and the report says what changed.

## Judging by eye

What the designer found after the checks were clean, and what fixed it.

- Look at every changed glyph whole and enlarged, filled black (`solid.py`, plus a window on each crossing and join), beside the nearest master. After the blocks were gone the designer still saw a 122° barb and 10–13° kinks in &, a crossing 30–47% too heavy, pinches, lumps and parts out of line, which combs and point views hid.
- Measure what you see (`widths.py`): disc widths per segment for the whole glyph, widths square to the edge along the stretch in question. Use both: rays read a taper too large near joins, the disc misses a long gentle one.
- Exactly master x k is not enough at Thin. The master's own small faults scale with it and show: it thins strokes into crossings (Light's & loop from 53 to 47 into the crossing; ¢'s bowl at its bar), its centre lines wiggle (bends of 5° and 9° just above &'s crossing became two lumps), its parts don't line up across a bar (¢). At Thin rebuild with `--smooth-centre --fill-crossings`. At Black the master's thinning would need strengthening instead (not tried).
- A stroke that passes under another is one stroke. Carry it through as one (both edges on one centre line: its own on each side, bridged by a curve matching both), and let the other stroke cut it. Every per-edge or per-side variant failed in the designer's eye: a pinch (each edge carried on alone; near a three-way join the master's centre line, seen from one edge, is dragged into the junction), a flare and "an inward curve" before the crossing (one curve fitted to each edge across it), "misaligned" across the leg (each side carried on alone), a kink where a single polynomial took over. Do it only where the stroke turns (over 20°): at &'s straight top crossing it put bumps where the fitted stretch met the rest.
- Build a glyph the way it is drawn. ¢ is c plus a bar in every master (c moved up 102, 99, 95 units), so at Thin it is the Thin c moved by the masters' trend, united with a straight bar at Light x k, fitted back onto ¢'s points (`union`). Fix the part first: the Thin c had barbs at both terminals. Rebuilt by itself, ¢ kept Light's misalignment across the bar.
- The designer's suggestions were the fixes ("take a C and overlay a /"; "imagine the curve continuing and do a boolean"). When a fix keeps failing, ask how they would draw it.
- Small pieces at corners: the master's piece x k at the new corner is right for chamfers and terminal flares (rebuilt as edges, c's flares collapsed); one running smoothly on from an inside corner is part of that edge (placed whole, it left a lump at &'s crossing). Nodes a unit or two off the master's exact meeting point land past it at Thin and curl: `union` snaps them onto the corner.
- Check symmetry and siblings, not only the strokes: the reviewer found the rebuilt C 14% heavier in its lower-left than its upper-left (Light: symmetric) and c and C overshooting 3–4 units past o and O (identical in Light). The first came from estimating the edge's direction at a smooth node by a chord a sixteenth into each segment, which leans to the longer one (Light's C has its upper-left quarter in one segment, its lower-left in two): each side's direction to second order, averaged, took it to 4%. Not the master's own direction (it disagreed with smoothed and carried-through samples: & went from 3 to 11 slows) and not at corners (B at Black folded). For the second, `--keep-limits left,right,top,bottom`.
- Group 4 (d, nine, the braces at Thin; B at Black) went the same way: `rebuild-outline --smooth-centre` evened d (x0.75–0.85 of Light x k) and nine (x0.65–1.44), where moving nodes had kinked them, with nine.tf synced to nine. The brace point first came out with a knee where the arm meets the point's short flat (Light's shape, hidden by its weight); `--smooth-centre 2` made it round and even. B's notch at Black went too, once straight edges were left out of the smoothing (smoothed with the curve they run into, the counters' flat sides tilted 1–2 units).
- Stroke ends: the designer saw the foot of the rebuilt Thin 9 taper (21 units at the baseline where Light x k gives 27). Next to an end cut the disc runs past the stroke's end, since the cut mustn't limit it, and comes out too big, which pulls the edge in; `rebuild-outline` now keeps, within half a stem of a cut, the width each side has just before it.
- Black & (the reviewer's barb, kinks, lump and notch) went with `rebuild-outline ampersand@900` (24 slows to 9) after two changes for heavy weights. A quarter of Bold's stem (30 units) took ordinary segments of Black's small counters for corner details: the short-piece length is now capped at 1.5% of the em. And heavier than the master, the little pieces where the arm and bowl meet the leg passed zero and turned round (3 blocks): a node that lands past a corner the rebuilt edges make now slides back along its edge, but only when its piece has nearly vanished or turned round and the edge runs on in the piece's direction (sliding every short piece put a knob-like step on B's waist). Keeping the crossing as derived (`--keep-nodes`) worked too but left a step where kept met rebuilt. Bold's shape copied onto each spot flattened the counter's tip; smoothing the centre lines turned handles round there. The designer then saw both counter tips stepped: Bold's little tip pieces, x 1.229 in Black's smaller counters. Bold's own tips copied as a whole (`copy-shape --similar`: turned and scaled between two good nodes) fixed both (4 slows left); per axis, the loop tip's two ends at nearly one height had blown the copy up. Counter tips at a heavy weight: rebuild, then copy the master's tip whole.
- Straight edges into a curve. Rounding the copied tip put 8–10° bends back into its straight edges, and the first try at straightening them (`straighten.py`) gave "a bit of a dip" to the designer: a turn of about 2° against the curve, where a straight edge meets the curve above it. An end node had been moved 1.4 units across the edge, with its handle, which bent that curve. Now ends only slide along the outline, `--aim` re-aims the curve's first handle into the edge's line (the curve moved under 0.6 units), and `--max-back` refuses an edge that turns against its curve. The master's step doesn't always fit: Black's left edge (30 units, three curves) was too short for Bold's (-4,5), which needs 38, and sliding node 9 up to make room flattened the side above; it took (-3,4). Measure each edge with its curve in every weight (`profile.py`): any turn back reads as a dip.
- Straight edges into a curve, in the drawn masters (group 6, with the designer's go-ahead). Straightening the edges of Light's and Regular's tip left a knee in Regular: the curve after the edge had its two handles almost on one point, so it turned in one spot (curvature peak 24.6, against about 10 in Light and Bold). A straight edge needs a curve that starts gently: end the edge earlier, put the curve's first handle on the edge's line, and let curvature rise from there (`fair.py --edge`; peak 8.0). Glyphs' smooth line-to-curve transition does the same: it "expands the curve into the line segment". Read the curvature per curve (`profile.py --curves`), not only the turn.
- An edit to one master must fit the others. Moving a smooth node in one master changes how it splits its handles; where the masters split it differently the weights in between kink although every master is smooth (Regular's & at 3.5:1 against 2:1 in Light and Bold: 3.4° at weight 350). Keep the split (`fair.py --ratio`), and check the weights in between unrounded (`between.py --raw`, then `profile.py`) before showing the masters.
- Look at the whole glyph before showing a whole-glyph sheet. The designer saw a flat crown in Regular's & ("a bit flattened") that was in the drawing, not in the fix: profile every curve of the glyph (`profile.py --curves` round each contour) for flat spots and turns back, and say which faults were already in the drawing.
- Check a master edit at the weights between the masters too, built (Medium, SemiBold): two constructions that each look right in their own master spike where they mix. The family's æ is generated (`letters.py`): the a-stem's corners went onto the e's outline only where the e came close (Bold), so the edge ran corner to corner through the e's eye. Bending the edge kept Bold's flat foot and was clean in every master, but Medium and SemiBold mixed it with Regular's construction and spiked where the stem meets the e. One construction in every weight (corners on the cut) fixed it, in the masters and in the generator.
- Read where a finding is before choosing the tool. The designer list said "@'s stem within 5 units of the ring"; the narrowest white was the notch under the a. "æ's eye a slit" came from a corner-to-corner edge, not from where the stem is cut.
- Measure strokes and gaps on flattened outlines (`fontlib.contour_polygon`), never on the point list: handles sit off the outline (a stem measured 13 units instead of 98). Fill renders by winding: an even-odd fill shows overlapping parts (æ, ø, ® inside its ring) as holes, and subtracting every counter from every fill blanks a part inside a ring.
- Practical: test a recipe change from the state just before that step (`awk '/^r rebuild-outline/{exit} {print}' recipe.sh` for a cut-down recipe): rebuilding an already rebuilt glyph isn't idempotent and showed flags a clean run didn't. Don't edit a recipe while it runs (bash reads scripts as it goes). On Windows and WSL drives file names are case-insensitive, so outputs for c and C overwrite each other; name them apart. Windows PowerShell 5.1's `Compress-Archive` writes backslashes into zip paths, which unpack wrong on Linux and macOS: zip sources with Python's `zipfile`.

## Op table details

- Joins only a few units off, with `fix.py --ops repredict --min-dev 1`: at Thin it moved a join 11 units sideways and tapered the cent sign's bar to 1 unit at the bowl (stroke-wedge), and a range over the bar's end pulled the bar 130 units in.
- A notch closed to a slit (@ at Black: the white under the a's bowl 3 units against Bold's 24; the stem's edge bent in above the hook): `copy-shape` beat `rebuild-outline` (which opened the notch but kinked the tail below it: a handle at tension 2.4) and `fair.py` (which keeps the direction into its first node, so it makes a corner smooth, and can't turn a curve into an edge from a node 3 units off it).
- A part shrunk to fit inside another (the R of ® at Black, x0.855 to clear the ring, stem 98 against ©'s C at 115): the full weight closed the R's own crotch at any size that fit; the designer chose stem 106, x1.065, centred.

## What the operations did

Regenerating Thin and Black from the three drawn masters: `merge` at 1 unit removed 27% of the nodes (3,584 to 2,600 per master), moved no master more than 0.93 units, and kept instances at 350, 500 and 600 within 0.8 units of the originals'. Thin at stem 28.4 and Black at 145, derived and put through the contrast step, from the three drawn masters only: 15 blocks at Thin (S, &, ¢, ?, ¿) and 18 at Black (€, the arrow, ¢, $, ?, ¿), all left for a designer and mostly too small to see at text size, and 511 slows in the two together against 1,157 in the earlier automated Thin/Black (same checks; lumps 833 against 1,511 over all five masters). The reviews found what the audit didn't: uneven weight and bumps in S, s and 3 (their drawn masters have dozens of nodes that slide between weights), the uneven rings of % and a thin f hook at Thin (curves whose weight shifts around them, which the contrast step's node-by-node estimates don't even out), a notch in Black B, the e's eye in Black æ nearly covered and the a of @ nearly touching its ring (both fixed later: æ in the generator's construction, @ with `copy-shape`), curls in the Black braces, the bar of Thin ð past the advance width. Put them on the designer list with a zoomed render (all weights side by side) each. In an earlier round that fixed the old Thin/Black in place (situation B), `collinear` on Thin and Black only cut kinks from 174 to 29. `repredict` on point ranges around five curls and barbs in Black removed every block the audit had found there. `triplet-ratio` cut kinks less than `collinear` (to 65). `harmonize`, after merging and rounding to whole units, didn't reduce scallops and raised ratio mismatches from 22 to 32 in the glyphs tested. Offer `triplet-ratio`, `harmonize` and `--snap-handles` only as separate choices, and keep them only where the audit and the renders improve.

## Extrapolation: what didn't work

Rebuilding a crotch from its edges' predicted angles (it moved the wrong way: a 1° change moves it 10 units), deriving a glyph at a closer stem (the nearest stem that gave an intact outline was 32–46 at Thin for S, &, ¢, ?, ¿ and 126–139 at Black; in a line of text the heavier or lighter glyph showed more than the small fold, and the contrast step couldn't bring it back without the checks getting worse; the exception is a fold that shows at text size with a clean stem close by: ® at Black, whose R ran into the ring, came out clean at 132 and only slightly lighter, so derive that glyph into a scratch folder with `--glyphs registered --stems 900=132` and copy it in, keeping the weight's advance width and left side bearing), setting the drawn masters' small-bend corners smooth (705 corners under 15° between curves, bending differently in each master; with smooth flags, collinear and a second merge the masters moved up to 1.9 units and the slows in Thin and Black rose from 539 to 844, curvature steps doubling: rounding leaves the handles bent, and a bent node flagged smooth extrapolates worse than a corner; leave them corners), extrapolating from the nearest two masters instead (Regular's strokes also grow faster than its stem: more strokes too thin, not fewer), and merging away tiny corner clusters (didn't hold within 1 or 2 units).
